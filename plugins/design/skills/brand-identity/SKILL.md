# Brand identity (senior methodology)

A brand is a **coherent point of view** expressed consistently — the ultimate
anti-generic discipline (`distinctive-design`). It starts from an idea, not a logo.
Ground type/color choices in real sources via `design-grounding`.

## Start from the idea, not the logo

- Define the brand's **core idea and personality** (3–5 adjectives), audience, and
  what makes it *different* — before any visual. A logo without a position is
  decoration. If you can't say the brand's one idea in a sentence, that's step one.

## The identity system

- **Logotype / mark:** often a considered logotype (the name in the right typeface,
  optically spaced) beats a generic abstract mark; keep it simple, legible small,
  monochrome-first, with clear space + min sizes (`iconography`).
- **Color:** a distinctive, ownable palette with roles and meaning
  (`color-systems`) — not a default. Brands are remembered by color.
- **Type:** a typographic voice — display + text families that carry the
  personality (`typography`), used consistently.
- **Imagery & motion:** a consistent illustration/photography style
  (`illustration-and-imagery`) and, if used, a signature motion (`motion-and-interaction`).

## Voice & tone

- How the brand *sounds* is part of identity: vocabulary, formality, humor,
  rhythm. Define voice (constant) and tone (shifts by context). Copy carries brand
  as much as visuals (`ux-principles`).

## Consistency = guidelines

- Encode the identity as usable **guidelines / tokens** (`design-systems`): logo
  usage, color, type, spacing, do/don't, voice examples. Consistency across
  touchpoints is what makes a brand feel real and trustworthy — the opposite of the
  generic, interchangeable look.

## Distinctive by design

- The whole point is to **not be interchangeable**. Push one idea; own a color, a
  typeface, a shape, a voice. Study real distinctive brands, avoid the category
  sameness (`design-research`).

## Reading it back

Reviewing a brand: is there a clear one-idea and personality? a distinctive,
consistent logo/color/type/voice system? guidelines to keep it consistent? is it
ownable or generic? Name where it's interchangeable and the fix. System encoding →
`design-systems`; marks → `iconography`.
